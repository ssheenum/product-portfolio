Industrial design Project 01

Un-Cream

A coffee cup that skims the milk skin.

Built for the Indian household where un-homogenized milk forms a film of milk-skin (malai or chaali).

DisciplineProduct · Form
ToolsFusion 360 · Sketching · CAD
01 - BACKSTORY

Milk that grows a skin

India is the world’s largest producer of milk, and most of it does not reach homes homogenized and pasteurized.

Milk is boiled in an open pot every morning.

What cools on top is a wrinkled, off-putting film of fat and protein called “malai”. And kids absolutely hate it.

02 - BRIEF

Enjoy the drink, skip the malai

How do we let people enjoy boiled coffee, chai, or hot chocolate, without ever meeting the malai?

The answer had to slip into existing morning rituals, not replace them. Take-away friendly. Dishwasher-safe. Cheap enough to be unremarkable.

“The dreaded malai, and the calcium deficiency it quietly creates.”
03 - IDEATION

Scrape, hinge, sieve

I sketched three mechanisms.
The sieve was the best option: fewer parts, no springs, easy to clean.

  1. 01ScrapeA sliding scraper that lifts the film off
  2. 02HingeA hinged flap that traps it against the lid
  3. 03SieveA stainless steel sieve that lets the liquid through and holds the film back
04 - RESULT

A cup with a snap-on mesh

Cup with a snap-on stainless steel mesh.

Industrial design Project 02

Tumble & Learn

A gentle first bump for a brand-new crawler.

A baby helmet that swaps non-biodegradable styrofoam for biodegradable air cushions, cutting helmet weight by 40%.

DisciplineProduct · Sustainability · CMF
ToolsFusion 360 · Soft goods · Material research
01 - THE PROBLEM

The runway starts the day they crawl

The moment a baby pushes up onto their hands and knees, the floor becomes a runway, and every coffee-table corner becomes a problem. Some children also self-soothe by head-banging into furniture and floors.

Parents are left to choose between bubble-wrapping their living room and watching every second.

“The moment they start crawling, the floor becomes a runway.”
“Head-banging is how some children learn to self-soothe.”
02 - MARKET RESEARCH

PROTECTIVE. LIGHT. 

Existing baby helmets are either rigid plastic shells borrowed from cycling, or bulky bonnets stuffed with EPS foam. They’re hot, heavy, and ugly enough that most parents quietly give up on them.

The brief: design something a baby will actually wear, and a parent will actually put on.

Market scan: rigid plastic shells and un-sustainable bulky styrofoam bonnets.
03 — Design approach

Shredded jute, sandwiched

Instead of one solid block of EPS foam, I designed a central cushion layer of finely shredded jute- light, sustainable, and surprisingly good at absorbing impact, sandwiched between a bio-based plant polymer outer shell and an organic cotton inner lining.

The jute is encapsulated in a thin cotton layer so it stays soft against the skin and breathes through the shell vents.

40%Lighter than a regular helmet

Final Design

The final form is a soft shell in playful prints: dinosaurs, clouds, a tiny galaxy.

Industrial design Project 03

Safe Tolls

A contact-free breath analyzer for the COVID era.

Drunk driving accounts for a startling share of the 6,871 accidents a year on the Old Mahabalipuram Road, one of the busiest highways in Chennai.

Safe Tolls turns the breath analyser into a contactless dispenser with a single-use breath pouch

DisciplineProduct · Mechanism · Public safety
ToolsMechanism design · chemistry · Sketching
01 - THE ROAD

OLD MAHABALIPURAM ROAD (OMR), CHENNAI

OMR Chennai is home to 480 IT companies and 132 hospitals, and it sees roughly 6,871 reported accidents every year. A disproportionate share of those involve alcohol.

The existing breathalyzer with mouthpiece used during police checks were completely halted during COVID due to its close-contact nature.

480IT companies
132Hospitals
6,871Accidents a year
02 - THE PROPOSAL

A contact-free test at every toll

Mandate an alcohol test at every toll plaza on OMR, but make it contact-free. No shared mouthpieces. No police officer in the driver’s breath path.

The user experience needed to feel less like a checkpoint and more like buying a vending-machine snack.

“The proposal: alcohol testing built into the toll-plaza ritual.”
03 - HOW IT WORKS

Pick up. Blow. Seal. Drop.

I designed a candy-style dispenser that hands the driver a sealed balloon. They blow into it, seal it, and drop it back through a chute.

Inside, a hot prick pops the balloon over a platinum electrode bath; the change in current tells you how much ethanol the breath carried. A UV chamber sterilizes everything between cycles.

  1. 01Pick upTake a sealed balloon from the dispenser
  2. 02BlowBreathe into it, no mouthpiece shared
  3. 03SealClose the balloon
  4. 04DropReturn it through the chute
04 - OUTCOME

Contactless, and it fits the booth

The mechanism is fully contactless, single-use, and designed to fit into a standard toll-booth footprint.

“Safe Tolls, reducing accidents due to human error on OMR.”

Industrial design Project 04

Xeyense

A pantry jar that knows what it’s holding.

A smart container with a load cell at the base and a tiny camera in the lid. Computer vision identifies what you’ve poured in; a companion app keeps a running log of what you’ve consumed today.

DisciplineProduct · IoT · Computer vision
ToolsHardware · Embedded ML · App design
01 - THE IDEA

Track what you eat, without tracking what you eat

Calorie apps fail because logging is a chore. Xeyense moves the logging into the container itself.

A load cell at the base measures weight changes, a camera in the lid recognises the contents on refill, and a companion app quietly keeps the daily ledger for you.

  1. 01Load cellAt the base, measures weight changes
  2. 02CameraIn the lid, recognises the contents on refill
  3. 03Companion appKeeps the daily ledger for you
